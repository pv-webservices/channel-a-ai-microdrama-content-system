## 2. Recommended changes

### 3–7s
Do not use a slow pan. Use two or three concise inserts linked by Maya's finger moving upward along the marks, with a moving box crossing the background. This creates both history and present-tense departure in the same beat.

### 7–12s
Keep the two-shot physically active: the landlord sets the tool down when Maya blocks him, then picks it up only after speaking. The object interaction prevents the dialogue from becoming a static exchange.

### 12–18s
Use Maya's choice as the visual escalation. After her father calls, she should briefly reach toward the marked strip as if considering staying, then reluctantly remove her hand and step toward the hall. The landlord waits for safe clearance before inserting the tool behind the trim. This turns the beat into a decision rather than waiting.

### 18–24s
Storyboard one clean visual proof: show a thin shadow gap opening behind the entire molding while the pencil marks remain untouched on its face. Avoid multiple confusing close-ups of tool mechanics.

### 24–30s
Keep the landlord's line, but make it optional in edit. The scene should still work muted: full marked strip in his hands + fresh replacement trim beside toolbox + Maya's changed expression.
