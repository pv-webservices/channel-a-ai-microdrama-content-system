## H1 — Only One

- **Hook type:** Immediate conflict / impossible choice
- **First visible frame:** Low basement shelf in vertical frame: an unplugged game console on one side, a worn cardboard memory box on the other, water spreading across the floor below them.
- **First action:** The older brother rushes into frame, stops between the two objects, and looks from one to the other.
- **Spoken/VO line:** **“He had time to save only one.”**
- **Unanswered question:** Which one will he save, and why?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** He must make a visible, consequential choice between the expensive console and the seemingly ordinary box.
- **Risk note:** Do not over-stage electrical danger. The console must be visibly unplugged / power-safe before the choice.
- **Human selection:** **APPROVED**

## H2 — Worth More

- **Hook type:** Contradiction
- **First visible frame:** Close visual contrast between the clean, expensive console and the battered cardboard box with a softened corner.
- **First action:** Water reaches the base of the shelf as his hand hovers between both.
- **Spoken/VO line:** “The cheaper box was worth more.”
- **Unanswered question:** What could be inside the box that matters more?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The contents must make the emotional value immediately understandable.
- **Risk note:** The line risks feeling overly explanatory if the visuals are already clear.

## H3 — The Grab

- **Hook type:** Visual mystery / shocking action
- **First visible frame:** His hand grabs the cardboard box while the newer console remains inches away.
- **First action:** He pulls the box against his chest and turns away from the console.
- **Spoken/VO line:** None in the first beat.
- **Unanswered question:** Why would he leave the more valuable object behind?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The box contents and sibling relationship must justify the choice.
- **Risk note:** Starting on the decision reduces suspense about what he will choose.

## H4 — Pipe Burst

- **Hook type:** Immediate action
- **First visible frame:** Water sprays from a failed pipe connection into the basement storage area.
- **First action:** He looks toward the two objects and moves quickly toward them.
- **Spoken/VO line:** “When the pipe burst, he didn’t run for what cost the most.”
- **Unanswered question:** What does he value more than the console?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The unexpected object must have credible emotional value.
- **Risk note:** Avoid disaster spectacle; the story is about the choice, not the plumbing failure.

## H5 — One Word

- **Hook type:** Minimal visual/text mystery
- **First visible frame:** Both objects framed with the brother centered between them as water advances.
- **First action:** His eyes move from console to box.
- **Spoken/VO line:** None.
- **On-screen text:** “ONE.”
- **Unanswered question:** One what? Which object?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The choice must occur almost immediately.
- **Risk note:** Text must not become the sole hook; the visual choice has to be readable muted.

## H6 — Memory Glimpse

- **Hook type:** Emotional statement
- **First visible frame:** A loose child’s drawing peeks from the cardboard box as water reaches it.
- **First action:** The brother notices the dampening corner and reacts.
- **Spoken/VO line:** “She thought nobody cared about that box.”
- **Unanswered question:** Why does the box matter, and what will he do?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The box must clearly belong to the younger sister and contain meaningful memories.
- **Risk note:** This reveals emotional significance earlier and weakens the value contrast slightly.

## H7 — Aftermath First

- **Hook type:** Consequence-first / unresolved outcome
- **First visible frame:** The younger sister stands at the basement doorway staring at the ruined console.
- **First action:** Her eyes move from the console to the dry memory box in her brother’s arms.
- **Spoken/VO line:** “She understood before he said a word.”
- **Unanswered question:** What happened, and what did he choose?
- **Promise the story must fulfill:** The story must quickly reconstruct the decision without feeling like a confusing flashback.
- **Risk note:** More editorial complexity for a 22–28 second runtime.

## Selected Hook

**H1 — “He had time to save only one.”**

### Selection rationale

H1 gives the clearest first-1–3-second contract:
- one character,
- two visible choices,
- immediate time pressure,
- one unanswered question,
- no false promise,
- payoff remains unrevealed.

It also works muted because the visual composition itself communicates the choice.
