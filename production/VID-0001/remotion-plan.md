## Why Candidate #1 failed

The edit architecture was too sparse:
- 7 still images for ~25 seconds
- average hold ~3.6 seconds per still
- only one 2-second narration line
- one caption cue only
- mostly subtle push/pan motion
- hard-cut-only transition strategy
- insufficient visual state changes to sell actions such as reach, reveal, decision, carry, and realization

This produced a slideshow feeling rather than cinematic short-form storytelling.

## Candidate #2 non-negotiable architecture

### 1. Denser image sequence
Use approximately **18–24 approved still frames** across the 25-second story.

Target:
- 2–4 stills per major scene/beat
- average visual state duration ~0.8–1.8 seconds
- longer holds only on intentional emotional beats

No AI video generation.

If needed, generate additional stills with the locked image policy:
1. Google Nano Banana 2 Lite
2. Google Nano Banana 2 for complex hand/two-character frames

### 2. Narrative voiceover across the story
Replace the single-line-only voice structure with a concise cinematic narration that carries the audience through:
- initial choice
- console-first instinct
- box tear
- childhood drawing reveal
- decision
- sacrifice
- sister's realization
- silent payoff

Target:
- ~45–60 spoken words
- approximately 18–22 seconds of narration
- intentional short silent spaces at the decision/payoff
- same approved voice route: Magnific MCP → ElevenLabs Turbo v2.5 → Grayson Kingsley

### 3. Dynamic captions
Use timed captions throughout narration rather than CAP-01 only.

Requirements:
- 5–8 caption groups
- 2–6 words per visual phrase where practical
- kinetic emphasis on emotionally important words
- no large static paragraph subtitles
- protect faces/hands/props
- caption position may change by scene

### 4. Stronger Remotion visual language
Use still-image motion more intentionally:
- fast controlled push on urgent beats
- crop/reframe changes between consecutive stills
- foreground/background parallax where source separation is viable
- flash/blur/whip-style transitions only when motivated
- match cuts based on hand direction, drawing position, box motion, or eyelines
- micro speed ramps on scale/position transforms
- reaction holds only where emotion benefits
- avoid repetitive identical Ken Burns movement

### 5. Editorial transitions
Do not use “hard cut everywhere” as the entire visual language.

Candidate #2 may use:
- hard cut
- match cut
- directional slide/whip transition
- short blur transition
- short dip-to-dark when emotionally justified
- scale-match transition
- audio-led cut

Transitions must remain fast and purposeful; no template-looking overuse.

### 6. Sound-edit synchronization
Audio must drive visual changes:
- cardboard rip triggers the reveal cut
- paper rustle supports drawing close-up
- footsteps match image progression toward stairs
- music enters at decision, not as generic background
- brief music reduction under high-information narration
- final beat gets controlled breathing room

### 7. Hook quality
First 3 seconds must contain:
- clear impossible choice
- fast visual progression, not one static frame
- voice + caption reinforcement
- visible console and memory box
- immediate question: what will he save?

### 8. Payoff quality
Final 5 seconds must clearly communicate:
- sister sees ruined/abandoned console
- sister sees the saved box/drawing
- she understands the sacrifice
- brother does not need to explain
- ending lands emotionally within one clear final beat
