## 1. Story Objective

Show a teenage older brother making an irreversible choice between a replaceable possession he values and his younger sister's irreplaceable memories. The audience should understand the sacrifice primarily through action and the sister's reaction, without a moral speech.

## 2. Beat Structure

| Beat | Time | Story function |
|---|---:|---|
| Hook | 0.0–2.0s | Establish one person, two objects, time pressure |
| Situation | 2.0–5.0s | His first instinct moves toward the console |
| Conflict | 5.0–8.0s | The memory box begins failing; a child’s drawing slips out |
| Escalation | 8.0–10.5s | He sees what the box actually contains and must decide now |
| Emotional turning point | 10.5–14.5s | He chooses the box |
| Payoff | 14.5–20.5s | The console is left behind; his sister sees both outcomes |
| Resolution | 20.5–25.0s | She sees the saved drawing, understands, and shares a silent look with him |

## 3. Timecoded Script

### 0.0–2.0s — HOOK

**VISUAL:**  
Vertical close-wide composition in a basement storage area. Water is already spreading across the floor beneath a low shelf. On one side: a clearly **unplugged** game console and controller. On the other: a worn cardboard box with softened lower corners. The 17-year-old older brother rushes into frame and stops between them.

**AUDIO:**  
Heavy water ambience. No music for the first beat.

**VO:**  
**“He had time to save only one.”**

---

### 2.0–5.0s — FIRST INSTINCT

**VISUAL:**  
His hand shoots toward the console. Water advances beneath the shelf. He grips the console edge—

Then hears wet cardboard tear.

**AUDIO:**  
Water + a sharp cardboard rip. No additional narration.

---

### 5.0–8.0s — MEMORY REVEAL

**VISUAL:**  
A child’s drawing slides partly from the weakening box. He catches it before it touches the water.

The drawing is simple and instantly readable: two siblings holding hands, with several other handmade notes/photos visible inside the box.

**AUDIO:**  
Water remains dominant. A small paper slap/fold sound as he catches it.

---

### 8.0–10.5s — DECISION

**VISUAL:**  
One fast eyeline sequence:

drawing → memory box → console.

No long reaction shot.

He releases the console.

**AUDIO:**  
Room tone narrows slightly. Music may enter here as a restrained low emotional bed, but no swell yet.

---

### 10.5–14.5s — CHOICE

**VISUAL:**  
He lifts the cardboard box with both arms, keeping the loose drawing pressed safely against it, and moves toward the stairs.

The console remains behind.

**AUDIO:**  
Wet footsteps, box handling, water.

---

### 14.5–20.5s — PAYOFF

**VISUAL:**  
At the basement stairs, his younger sister appears.

She first sees the console still sitting in the spreading water zone.

Then her eyes move to the dry box in her brother’s arms.

He does not explain.

**AUDIO:**  
Music becomes slightly warmer but remains understated. No dialogue.

---

### 20.5–25.0s — RESOLUTION

**VISUAL:**  
The sister pulls the saved drawing from the top of the box.

She looks at it, then at her brother.

He gives the smallest shrug, as if the choice was obvious.

She presses the drawing back against the box and steps beside him.

**CUT immediately.**

**AUDIO:**  
Water ambience continues underneath. Let the final beat breathe for less than a second. No moral line. No outro.

## 5. Continuity Requirements

- The console must be visibly unplugged / electrically safe from the first shot.
- The same worn cardboard box must remain identifiable throughout.
- The child’s drawing must be visually consistent once revealed.
- Box damage should increase slightly from hook to decision but remain carryable.
- The console stays in the same position after he releases it.
- The sister must see the console before seeing the saved box for the payoff to read correctly.
- Do not show a replacement console or external reward.

## 6. Risks / Open Questions

- AI-generated water interaction can create continuity errors; keep water level/movement simple.
- The drawing should communicate “sibling memory” visually without depending on tiny readable text.
- The brother’s reaction must be brief; lingering too long would weaken urgency.
- The sister’s final reaction should stay restrained and human rather than exaggerated crying/hugging.
- Production should avoid any visual that implies entering energized flood water.
