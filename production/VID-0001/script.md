# VID-0001 / STORY-0001 — Script

**Title:** The Flooded Box  
**Script version:** v2 — retention optimized  
**Status:** AWAITING HUMAN SCRIPT APPROVAL  
**Target runtime:** ~25 seconds  
**Selected working hook:** H1 — Immediate impossible choice  
**Voice mode:** Minimal narrator + visual storytelling

## 1. Story objective
Make the audience understand, through one irreversible choice, that the brother values his younger sister’s irreplaceable memories more than his own expensive possession—without explaining the moral.

## 2. Beat table

| Beat | Time | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:03 | Present two threatened objects and one impossible choice. |
| Conflict | 0:03–0:07 | Brother initially commits to saving the console; sibling memory interrupts the choice. |
| Choice | 0:07–0:12 | He abandons the console and saves the memory box. |
| Emotional turning point | 0:12–0:17 | Sister arrives and understands that he left his own possession behind. |
| Payoff | 0:17–0:22 | The saved drawing confirms why the box mattered. |
| Resolution | 0:22–0:25 | Quiet physical acknowledgment; end immediately. |

## 3. Timecoded script

### 0:00–0:03 — HOOK
**VISUAL:** Water spreads across the basement floor toward a new game console on a low shelf and a worn cardboard memory box. The console’s power cable is visibly unplugged. The older brother runs into frame and stops between them.

**AUDIO:** Water rush + pipe hiss.  
**VO:** “He had time to save only one.”

### 0:03–0:07 — CONFLICT
**VISUAL:** He immediately grabs the console. As it lifts, a damp childhood drawing slips from the cardboard box and lands face-up near his shoe: two young siblings holding hands.

**AUDIO:** Console scrape + wet paper slap.

### 0:07–0:12 — CHOICE
**VISUAL:** He looks down once. He lowers the console, crosses to the box, and lifts it with both arms. Water reaches the console shelf as he turns toward the stairs.

**AUDIO:** Cardboard creak, footsteps through shallow water. No narration.

### 0:12–0:17 — TURNING POINT
**VISUAL:** His younger sister appears at the top of the basement stairs just as he reaches the first dry step. She sees her old box in his arms, then looks past him at the wet console below.

**AUDIO:** Water recedes slightly in the mix; room tone opens.

### 0:17–0:22 — PAYOFF
**VISUAL:** He sets the box down. The same childhood drawing sits on top beside a few handmade cards. She picks up the drawing, recognizes it, then looks back at the console and finally at him.

**AUDIO:** Paper rustle. A restrained tonal bed may enter.

### 0:22–0:25 — RESOLUTION
**VISUAL:** She pulls the box against her chest and reaches out to squeeze his wet hand. He exhales. Cut immediately.

**AUDIO:** Distant water only. No moral line.

## 5. Continuity requirements
- Console must remain the same model and position until the brother moves it.
- Console power cable must be visibly unplugged before water reaches the shelf.
- Memory box must retain the same worn stickers/markings.
- The same childhood drawing that slips out must appear in the payoff.
- Drawing should communicate “siblings” visually without relying on readable text.
- Brother’s clothing wetness must progress consistently from shoes/lower legs upward only as justified.
- Sister enters from the dry stair side.
- Water level/wetness must progress consistently.
- Keep the box dry enough after rescue that the saved contents remain credible.

## 6. Risks / open questions
- The childhood drawing must read clearly in under a second.
- Asset generation must preserve exact console/box/drawing continuity.
- Water movement should feel urgent but not like a disaster sequence.
- The sister’s final reaction should remain restrained rather than overly sentimental.
- Final timing may require frame-level adjustment after voice and visual assets exist.
