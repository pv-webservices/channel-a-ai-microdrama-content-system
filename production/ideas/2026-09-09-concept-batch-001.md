## C01 — The Camera Down

- **Working title:** The Camera Down
- **Premise:** At a small awards ceremony, a father reaches across the aisle and lowers another parent’s phone just as his 19-year-old daughter walks onstage for her first public performance.
- **Main character:** Father of a young performer who has recently struggled with public online exposure.
- **Central conflict:** The filming parent thinks the father is being rude and controlling; the daughter freezes when she notices the phone is live-streaming.
- **Emotional trigger:** The daughter had explicitly asked her family not to let the performance be posted live.
- **Twist/payoff:** The father points to the live-stream icon and quietly asks the other parent to record locally instead. The stream stops; his daughter sees the screen go dark, exhales, and continues.
- **Estimated duration:** 28–34s
- **Content pillar:** family / social behavior / trust
- **Why viewers may care:** It turns a small public conflict into a question of consent and protection without a speech.
- **Visual potential:** Stage entrance, phone live icon, hand lowering phone, daughter eye contact, screen switching off, relieved continuation.
- **Diversity note:** Privacy/consent conflict; no rescue, money, grief object, or punishment-based karma.

## C02 — The Flooded Box

- **Working title:** The Flooded Box
- **Premise:** A pipe bursts in a basement storage room and a teenager has seconds to choose between saving his new game console and a cardboard box filled with his younger sister’s childhood drawings, school notes, and handmade cards.
- **Main character:** 17-year-old older brother.
- **Central conflict:** Rising water makes it impossible to carry both items at once.
- **Emotional trigger:** The console is expensive and replaceable; the sister’s box looks cheap but contains years of irreplaceable memories.
- **Twist/payoff:** He grabs the cardboard box and leaves the console behind. Later his sister sees the soaked console through the doorway and realizes what he chose without him explaining it.
- **Estimated duration:** 22–28s
- **Content pillar:** siblings / sacrifice / difficult choices
- **Why viewers may care:** The choice is instantly understandable and reveals priorities through action rather than dialogue.
- **Visual potential:** Water spreading, power unplug, console, fragile cardboard box, split-second choice, soaked console, sister reaction.
- **Diversity note:** No misunderstanding twist; the story is driven by a visible irreversible choice.

## C03 — The Spare Helmet

- **Working title:** The Spare Helmet
- **Premise:** A teenager catches his older brother swapping their bicycle helmets before school and assumes he is taking the newer one for himself.
- **Main character:** 15-year-old younger brother.
- **Central conflict:** He angrily switches them back because he believes his brother always takes the better gear.
- **Emotional trigger:** The younger brother feels treated as secondary.
- **Twist/payoff:** The older brother flips the older helmet over and reveals a hairline crack near the rear shell. He puts the safer helmet back on the younger brother and walks toward the bus carrying the cracked one instead of riding.
- **Estimated duration:** 24–30s
- **Content pillar:** siblings / sacrifice / trust
- **Why viewers may care:** Ordinary sibling friction becomes a concrete safety sacrifice.
- **Visual potential:** Helmet swap, crack close-up, defensive body language, bus stop, unused bicycle.
- **Diversity note:** Safety-based sibling sacrifice, distinct from sports sabotage and object-preservation stories.

## C04 — The Group Photo

- **Working title:** The Group Photo
- **Premise:** At a grandmother’s birthday, a teenage girl repeatedly refuses to join the family photo while relatives grow irritated.
- **Main character:** 16-year-old granddaughter.
- **Central conflict:** Her family thinks she is being difficult during an important family moment.
- **Emotional trigger:** A shy stepbrother has been asked to take every photo and is missing from all of them.
- **Twist/payoff:** She takes the phone from him, places it on a timer stand, pulls him into the group, and only then joins the picture herself.
- **Estimated duration:** 20–26s
- **Content pillar:** family / inclusion / social behavior
- **Why viewers may care:** Being present in family memories is a simple, recognizable form of belonging.
- **Visual potential:** Repeated camera framing, empty position, phone handoff, timer countdown, final wider group.
- **Diversity note:** Inclusion story with no villain and no secret rescue.

## C05 — The Name Card

- **Working title:** The Name Card
- **Premise:** Minutes before a student design exhibition opens, a young designer returns to her table and sees her teammate switching the printed name cards beneath two models so the stronger project appears to be theirs.
- **Main character:** 22-year-old design student.
- **Central conflict:** She has to decide whether to confront the teammate publicly just before judging begins.
- **Emotional trigger:** The betrayal rewrites months of visible work in seconds.
- **Twist/payoff:** She silently restores the correct cards and turns on the project’s required process screen, which shows a time-lapse of who built each model. The teammate sees the footage playing as the judges approach.
- **Estimated duration:** 26–34s
- **Content pillar:** betrayal / trust / consequences
- **Why viewers may care:** Credit theft is broadly relatable, and the consequence comes from existing evidence rather than revenge.
- **Visual potential:** Swapped labels, model close-ups, time-lapse screen, teammate reaction, judges entering.
- **Diversity note:** Actual betrayal rather than a misunderstanding; consequence is procedural, not humiliating.

## C06 — The Empty Carriage

- **Working title:** The Empty Carriage
- **Premise:** On a nearly empty evening train, a woman becomes wary when another woman deliberately sits beside her despite dozens of open seats.
- **Main character:** 27-year-old commuter.
- **Central conflict:** The closeness feels intrusive until the stranger starts a casual conversation and subtly blocks the aisle side.
- **Emotional trigger:** The main character has noticed the same man move between carriages whenever she moves.
- **Twist/payoff:** At the next stop, the man sees the two women together and exits. The stranger waits one beat, asks “You good?”, then leaves at the following station without asking for thanks.
- **Estimated duration:** 25–32s
- **Content pillar:** unexpected kindness / social behavior / trust
- **Why viewers may care:** It dramatizes quiet situational awareness and help that preserves dignity.
- **Visual potential:** Empty seats, repeated background figure, seat choice, tote blocking aisle, platform exit, restrained eye contact.
- **Diversity note:** Stranger-safety story based on social reading, not physical rescue or confrontation.

## C07 — The Closed Curtain

- **Working title:** The Closed Curtain
- **Premise:** A stage manager abruptly drops the curtain in the middle of an actor’s best scene, causing the audience to boo and the actor to stare backstage in disbelief.
- **Main character:** 30-year-old community-theater actor.
- **Central conflict:** The actor thinks months of work were ruined by an unexplained technical decision.
- **Emotional trigger:** The interruption happens at the emotional peak of the performance.
- **Twist/payoff:** Seconds after the curtain falls, a loose overhead scenic piece crashes onto the stage behind it. The actor looks toward the stage manager, who is already checking the rig rather than seeking credit.
- **Estimated duration:** 20–25s
- **Content pillar:** difficult choices / trust / responsibility
- **Why viewers may care:** It turns apparent sabotage into a split-second safety decision with visible proof.
- **Visual potential:** Stage performance, curtain drop, boos, falling scenic piece, silent realization.
- **Diversity note:** High-motion workplace/theater safety story with almost no dialogue.

## C08 — The Mismatched Shoes

- **Working title:** The Mismatched Shoes
- **Premise:** A student arrives at a school presentation wearing two visibly different sneakers after leaving home in a rush, and a small group begins laughing.
- **Main character:** 15-year-old student.
- **Central conflict:** He considers skipping the presentation rather than walking to the front of the room.
- **Emotional trigger:** The mistake becomes public at the exact moment he needs confidence.
- **Twist/payoff:** His closest friend, who wears the same shoe size, quietly swaps one sneaker with him. They both walk into the presentation wearing mismatched pairs.
- **Estimated duration:** 18–24s
- **Content pillar:** friendship / social behavior / kindness
- **Why viewers may care:** Small embarrassment can feel enormous at that age; solidarity changes the social meaning without a speech.
- **Visual potential:** Shoe close-up, laughter glance, doorway hesitation, silent shoe swap, both walking forward.
- **Diversity note:** No hidden motive or later explanation; payoff is immediate solidarity.

## C09 — The Missed Call

- **Working title:** The Missed Call
- **Premise:** During a job interview waiting period, a young woman keeps declining her mother’s repeated calls because she assumes it is another trivial check-in.
- **Main character:** 25-year-old daughter.
- **Central conflict:** She must choose between staying focused on a career-defining moment and responding to someone who calls her too often.
- **Emotional trigger:** After the interview she sees a short voicemail preview: “It bloomed.”
- **Twist/payoff:** She calls back expecting bad news, but her mother simply turns the camera toward a plant they had been trying to revive together for months. The daughter laughs, then stays on the call while walking home.
- **Estimated duration:** 24–30s
- **Content pillar:** relationships / regret / second chances
- **Why viewers may care:** It uses everyday neglect and repair without a death fake-out.
- **Visual potential:** Repeated call screen, interview hallway, voicemail preview, video call, blooming plant, walking-home continuation.
- **Diversity note:** Quiet emotional story; no catastrophe, rescue, or deception.

## C10 — The Last Clean Shirt

- **Working title:** The Last Clean Shirt
- **Premise:** On the same morning, a single father has a job interview and his teenage son has a scholarship presentation, but a laundromat failure leaves only one properly pressed white shirt.
- **Main character:** 16-year-old son.
- **Central conflict:** The son expects his father to keep the shirt because the interview affects the whole family.
- **Emotional trigger:** Both opportunities matter, and neither character can solve the problem without giving something up.
- **Twist/payoff:** The father hands the shirt to his son and attends his own interview in a clean dark sweater and old jacket. Before getting on the bus, the son removes his tie and gives it to his father, so both leave with part of the formal outfit.
- **Estimated duration:** 30–36s
- **Content pillar:** family / sacrifice / difficult choices
- **Why viewers may care:** The conflict is ordinary, believable, and emotionally reciprocal rather than one-sided.
- **Visual potential:** One hanging white shirt, two appointment reminders, shirt handoff, tie return, opposite buses.
- **Diversity note:** Reciprocal family sacrifice; no reward outcome is shown or guaranteed.
