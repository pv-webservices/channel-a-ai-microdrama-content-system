## C01 — The Height Marks

- **Working title:** The Height Marks
- **Premise:** While a family moves out of an apartment, an 11-year-old sees the landlord scraping years of pencil height marks from a doorframe and believes their family history is being erased.
- **Main character:** 11-year-old child leaving the only home they remember.
- **Central conflict:** The child tries to stop the landlord from removing the marks while the adults are carrying boxes outside.
- **Emotional trigger:** Each pencil line represents a birthday, sibling, or family memory.
- **Twist/payoff:** The landlord is not destroying the marked section. He carefully removes the narrow strip of molding, replaces it with new trim, and hands the original marked strip to the family so they can take it with them.
- **Estimated duration:** 32–38s
- **Content pillar:** family / unexpected kindness
- **Why viewers may care:** Moving homes and losing physical traces of childhood are widely understandable emotional stakes.
- **Visual potential:** Empty apartment, stacked boxes, pencil marks, scraping tool, trim removal, final handoff.
- **Diversity note:** Memory-preservation story; no money/status conflict and no punishment-based karma structure.

## C02 — The Missing Shoe

- **Working title:** The Missing Shoe
- **Premise:** Minutes before a school relay, a runner's racing shoe disappears and she suspects the reserve teammate who would take her place.
- **Main character:** 17-year-old relay runner.
- **Central conflict:** She confronts the reserve teammate for sabotaging her chance to compete.
- **Emotional trigger:** The apparent betrayal happens immediately before an important race.
- **Twist/payoff:** She finds the teammate under the bleachers repairing a split sole the runner had not noticed, using her own spare laces and missing part of her warm-up to make the shoe safe.
- **Estimated duration:** 30–35s
- **Content pillar:** friendship / sacrifice
- **Why viewers may care:** Competition creates believable suspicion, while the payoff turns rivalry into practical loyalty.
- **Visual potential:** Track lanes, missing shoe, cracked sole, repair close-ups, starting-line urgency.
- **Diversity note:** Sports-based friendship conflict driven by safety and sacrifice rather than wealth, romance, or public humiliation.

## C03 — The Fake Tip

- **Working title:** The Fake Tip
- **Premise:** A content creator records himself leaving a huge cash tip for a restaurant server, then quietly takes the money back when his camera stops.
- **Main character:** 24-year-old server.
- **Central conflict:** The server watches the creator receive praise for generosity built on a lie.
- **Emotional trigger:** Public praise contrasts with the server's private humiliation.
- **Twist/payoff:** A second diner's phone, filming a birthday at the next table, catches the cash being taken back in the reflection behind him; the creator sees the uncut footage when the diner shows the server.
- **Estimated duration:** 30–38s
- **Content pillar:** greed / consequences / social behavior
- **Why viewers may care:** Performative kindness and authenticity are recognizable social-media tensions.
- **Visual potential:** Cash on table, creator camera, reflection reveal, server reaction.
- **Diversity note:** Consequence comes from documented behavior rather than supernatural karma or a rich-vs-poor reversal.

## C04 — The Yanked Backpack

- **Working title:** The Yanked Backpack
- **Premise:** On a crowded escalator, a woman suddenly yanks a stranger's backpack off his shoulder and he assumes she is trying to steal it.
- **Main character:** 28-year-old commuter.
- **Central conflict:** He grabs the bag back angrily as the escalator reaches the landing.
- **Emotional trigger:** A seemingly aggressive public act with no explanation.
- **Twist/payoff:** The loose backpack strap is already caught between the escalator steps; after she pulls the bag free, the dangling end is visibly dragged into the mechanism.
- **Estimated duration:** 20–25s
- **Content pillar:** unexpected kindness / social behavior
- **Why viewers may care:** The opening is instantly legible and the payoff recontextualizes the same physical action.
- **Visual potential:** Tight escalator framing, caught strap, sudden pull, mechanical danger, silent realization.
- **Diversity note:** Action-led safety story with minimal dialogue and no sentimental backstory.

## C05 — The Covered Painting

- **Working title:** The Covered Painting
- **Premise:** A young artist discovers that a gallery custodian covers only her painting with cloth every morning before opening.
- **Main character:** 26-year-old emerging artist.
- **Central conflict:** She believes the custodian dislikes or is sabotaging her work and confronts him.
- **Emotional trigger:** Her first public exhibition feels quietly undermined.
- **Twist/payoff:** He shows her the failed skylight shade and a slightly faded exposed edge; he has been shielding the painting from direct morning sun until maintenance repairs the shade.
- **Estimated duration:** 28–35s
- **Content pillar:** difficult choices / unexpected kindness
- **Why viewers may care:** The conflict is professional pride versus unseen care, with an evidence-based reveal.
- **Visual potential:** Cloth drop, artwork, shaft of sunlight, faded edge, restrained expressions.
- **Diversity note:** Art-world setting and conservation problem; payoff is protective competence, not charity or revenge.

## C06 — The Empty Lunchbox

## C07 — The Last Bouquet

- **Working title:** The Last Bouquet
- **Premise:** A frantic customer offers far above asking price for the final white bouquet in a flower shop, but the florist refuses to sell it.
- **Main character:** 35-year-old florist.
- **Central conflict:** The customer accuses her of being unreasonable because he needs the flowers to repair a relationship immediately.
- **Emotional trigger:** She must absorb anger while protecting an unseen prior commitment.
- **Twist/payoff:** As he exits, a grieving family arrives to collect the bouquet reserved for a funeral; he sees why money could not override the promise.
- **Estimated duration:** 25–32s
- **Content pillar:** moral dilemma / relationships
- **Why viewers may care:** It asks whether urgency and money should override an existing human commitment.
- **Visual potential:** Last bouquet, cash pushed forward, florist refusing, funeral pickup reveal.
- **Diversity note:** Moral-choice story without deception, theft, or revenge.

## C08 — The Locked Bicycle

- **Working title:** The Locked Bicycle
- **Premise:** Just before an unofficial downhill race, a teenager finds her bicycle chain-locked by her closest friend.
- **Main character:** 18-year-old rider.
- **Central conflict:** She believes her friend is sabotaging her after previously warning her not to race.
- **Emotional trigger:** The race is about to start and the interference feels controlling.
- **Twist/payoff:** The friend shows her a nearly severed brake cable he found moments earlier; he locked the bike because she had already dismissed his warning and was about to leave.
- **Estimated duration:** 25–30s
- **Content pillar:** friendship / difficult choices
- **Why viewers may care:** It pits autonomy against urgent safety in a recognizable friendship conflict.
- **Visual potential:** Locked wheel, countdown, cracked cable close-up, anger collapsing into realization.
- **Diversity note:** Safety-based moral conflict; no romantic misunderstanding and no reward/punishment ending.

## C09 — The Unclaimed Medal

- **Working title:** The Unclaimed Medal
- **Premise:** A relay runner steps off the podium after her team wins and leaves the medal hanging from the official's hand.
- **Main character:** 17-year-old athlete.
- **Central conflict:** Her coach thinks she is disrespecting the event after the team's substitute finished the final leg.
- **Emotional trigger:** The win feels incomplete because the injured original teammate trained for months but was forced out moments before the race.

- **Estimated duration:** 24–30s
- **Content pillar:** friendship / sacrifice
- **Why viewers may care:** Recognition, fairness, and team identity create a clean emotional conflict.
- **Visual potential:** Empty podium position, medal close-up, crutches/sideline, shared ending.
- **Diversity note:** Recognition-based payoff rather than rescue, money, romance, or revenge.

## C10 — The Returned Jacket

- **Working title:** The Returned Jacket
- **Premise:** While volunteering at a clothing donation center, a man recognizes a patched school jacket that belonged to a classmate he bullied years earlier.
- **Main character:** 30-year-old man confronting an old memory.
- **Central conflict:** He initially wants to hide the jacket because the torn patch reminds him of damage he caused.
- **Emotional trigger:** The object makes his past behavior physical and undeniable.

- **Estimated duration:** 35–42s
- **Content pillar:** regret / second chances
- **Why viewers may care:** It treats remorse as action rather than a speech demanding forgiveness.
- **Visual potential:** Recognizable patch, memory flash fragments, sewing repair, anonymous note in pocket.
- **Diversity note:** Internal regret story with no public exposure and no guarantee of forgiveness.

## C11 — The Cracked Recorder

- **Working title:** The Cracked Recorder
- **Premise:** A teenager walks in on his father prying apart an old voice recorder that contains the last recordings of his late mother.
- **Main character:** 16-year-old son.

- **Emotional trigger:** Grief turns a technical repair into an apparent act of erasure.

- **Content pillar:** family / regret / grief
- **Why viewers may care:** Fear of losing a loved one's final traces is immediate and human.
- **Visual potential:** Cracked casing, swollen battery, memory card, waveform transfer, restrained father/son reaction.
- **Diversity note:** Grief story driven by preservation and technical urgency rather than death reveal or supernatural sentiment.

## C12 — The Spare Chair

- **Working title:** The Spare Chair
- **Premise:** An elderly café regular notices a server repeatedly moving the unused second chair away from her table and begins to think the staff want her to stop lingering alone.
- **Main character:** 72-year-old widow.
- **Central conflict:** She finally confronts the server after feeling quietly pushed out of her routine.
- **Emotional trigger:** The empty chair carries the absence of her late partner.
- **Twist/payoff:** The server has been placing that chair beside another solitary regular's table during the busiest hour; when the widow follows it, the other customer silently offers the sugar bowl and leaves the chair open for her.
- **Estimated duration:** 30–38s
- **Content pillar:** loneliness / second chances / unexpected kindness
- **Why viewers may care:** Small rituals of loneliness are visually simple and emotionally recognizable.
- **Visual potential:** Empty chair motif, repeated table routine, chair movement, final shared table.
- **Diversity note:** Quiet social connection rather than dramatic reversal, punishment, or family reconciliation.
