# VID-0002 — Approved Concept

**Story ID:** STORY-0002  
**Working title:** The Height Marks  
**Status:** HUMAN CONCEPT APPROVED — 2026-09-08  
**Target platforms:** YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels  
**Target duration:** 32–38 seconds  
**Default framing:** 9:16  
**Content pillar:** family / unexpected kindness  
**Emotional progression:** alarm → resistance → uncertainty → realization → tenderness

## Premise

While a family moves out of the apartment where their children grew up, 11-year-old **Maya** sees the landlord scraping at years of pencil height marks on the kitchen doorway. She believes he is erasing the only physical record of their childhood from the home.

## Main character

Maya, 11, leaving the only home she clearly remembers.

## Central conflict

Maya tries to stop the landlord from removing the height marks while her parents are occupied carrying the final boxes outside.

## Emotional trigger

The marks are not decoration to Maya. Each line contains a name, age, date, and a piece of family history that cannot be recreated accurately later.

## Twist / payoff

The landlord is not sanding the marks away. He is cutting the narrow marked strip of door trim free from the wall. He has already prepared a replacement strip so the apartment can be repaired while the family takes the original height-mark strip with them.

## Resolution

He places the marked strip across the final moving box. Maya runs one finger over the oldest line, then helps carry it out.

## Why viewers may care

Moving often turns ordinary objects into emotional anchors. The conflict is understandable before dialogue, and the payoff reinterprets an apparently destructive action as practical kindness.

## Visual potential

- nearly empty apartment
- stacked moving boxes
- close vertical line of pencil marks and dates
- child's hand protecting the marks
- scraper / trim tool approaching
- trim separating intact from the wall
- clean replacement molding visible nearby
- final marked strip carried out with the family's boxes

## Originality check

This story is driven by memory preservation and misinterpreted practical action. It does not depend on wealth/status humiliation, punishment-based karma, romance, a cash reward, or a generic moral speech.

## Production risks

1. The landlord's tool/action must initially look like plausible removal work without visually implying deliberate destruction beyond repair.
2. The reveal must make it obvious that the marked strip remains intact.
3. Replacement trim should be visible briefly so the landlord's motivation is self-explanatory.
4. Avoid excessive dialogue; the object and actions should carry the story.
